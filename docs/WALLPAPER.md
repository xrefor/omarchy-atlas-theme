# Ember Seam

## Historical source-generation record

The prompt below records the first Ember Seam generated for ATLAS on September
9, 2026, with no reference images. That source was a native 1672 × 941 image and
was not upscaled. It is not the exact prompt or provenance record for the current
`backgrounds/atlas-ember-seam.png`: Ember Seam was rerendered and resized for the
3440 × 1440 distribution on September 18. See the [showcase media
notes](media/README.md) for the known current processing record.

## Final generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: high-resolution desktop wallpaper for the ATLAS Linux theme, wide 16:9 landscape, ideally 3840x2160.
Primary request: minimal tech, science fiction, near future. Original artwork.
Scene/subject: an abstract close architectural study of a near-future industrial machine enclosure: a few enormous precision-cut graphite-black planes and recessed seams, one narrow ember-orange illuminated slit receding into darkness. Suggest tangible engineered technology without describing a recognizable device. A restrained asymmetrical composition with a small focal area and generous dark negative space; large calm shapes readable at a distance.
Style/medium: sophisticated cinematic hard-surface 3D concept render, photoreal materials, exceptionally minimal and quiet. Subtle bevels, fine matte carbon and anodized metal texture, sparse manufacturing details. Not a busy spaceship interior.
Lighting/mood: very low-key, soft grazing illumination just enough to distinguish the dark planes. Restrained warm glow from the single orange seam; no blown-out highlights. Atmospheric but crisp, premium industrial design, plausible near-future technology.
Color palette: predominantly warm carbon black #100e0c and #0a0908, muted warm charcoal #1c1814 and #3a342c; small controlled heated-orange #ff5a12 accent, faint warm ivory #d6cfc4 reflections. Keep most of the image dark, orange below 5 percent of the frame.
Constraints: full-bleed wallpaper, no text, no lettering, no logos, no watermark, no borders, no desktop/UI mockup, no HUD overlays. No people, no planets, no starfields, no flying vehicles, no neon cyan or purple, no generic circuit-board patterns, no dense greebles. Produce one finished image.
